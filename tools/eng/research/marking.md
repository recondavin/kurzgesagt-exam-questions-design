## (a) The official criteria for assessment (all of Paper 1 and Paper 2)

From MS2025, p. 2 (the same wording appears in every scheme from 2012 to 2026):

> "The tasks set for candidates in both Paper 1 and Paper 2 will be assessed in accordance with the following criteria:
> • Clarity of Purpose (P) 30% of the marks available for the task
> • Coherence of Delivery (C) 30% of the marks available for the task
> • Efficiency of Language Use (L) 30% of the marks available for the task
> • Accuracy of Mechanics (M) 10% of the marks available for the task"

> "Each answer will be in the form of a response to a specific task, requiring candidates
> • To display a clear and purposeful engagement with the set task
> • To sustain the response in an appropriate manner over the entire answer
> • To manage and control language appropriate to the task
> • To display levels of accuracy in spelling and grammar appropriate to the required/chosen register."

> "Given the primacy of Clarity of Purpose (P), marks awarded for either Coherence of Delivery (C) or Efficiency of Language Use (L) cannot exceed the marks awarded for Clarity of Purpose."

MS2025, Appendix 1, *Criteria for Assessment*. The table is reproduced here row by row:

| Criterion | What it is | What it covers | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clarity of Purpose** | "Engagement with the set task" | "e.g. relevance, focus, originality, freshness, evidence of critical literacy (where appropriate), clear aim, understanding of genre" | 30 |
| **Coherence of Delivery** | "Ability to sustain the response over the entire answer" | "Where appropriate: continuity of argument, sequencing, management of ideas, choice of reference, use of examples, engagement with texts, control of register and shape, creative modelling" | 30 |
| **Efficiency of Language Use** | "Management and control of Language to achieve clear communication" | "e.g. vocabulary, syntax, sentence patterns, punctuation appropriate to the register, use of lively interesting phrasing, energy, style, fluency appropriate to the task" | 30 |
| **Accuracy of Mechanics** | "Spelling / Grammar" | "e.g. appropriate levels of accuracy in spelling / grammatical patterns appropriate to the register" | 10 |

How the criteria are worded for the comparative itself (MS2025, every 70-mark and 40-mark comparative question; a 30-mark part leaves out the comparison items):

> "P: Focus and relevance, evidence of critical literacy
> In awarding marks for P, consider the following:
> - evidence of understanding of the mode [Theme or Issue / Cultural Context / General Vision and Viewpoint]
> - focus on: [the exact task set]
> - evidence of effective comparison within the mode [...]
> C: Sustained focus & relevance/appropriate management and sequencing of ideas/coherence/sustained comparative focus/apt use of examples, engagement with texts, etc.
> L: Language managed and controlled to achieve clear communication throughout/fluency quality of expression, use of comparative language, etc.
> M: Accuracy of mechanics"

## (b) How the marks are split

### Single 70-mark question: discrete criteria, P 21 / C 21 / L 21 / M 7

Every 70-mark comparative question in MS2012 to MS2026 is headed "Mark ex 70 by reference to the criteria for assessment using the breakdown of marks below" and is followed by:

```
P 21
C 21
L 21
M 7
```

Grade grid for 70 marks (MS2025 p. 34, also Appendix 2 "Discrete Criteria"):

| | H1 | H2 | H3 | H4 | H5 | H6 | H7 | H8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100% (70) | 70–63 | 62–56 | 55–49 | 48–42 | 41–35 | 34–28 | 27–21 | 20–0 |
| 30% (P, C or L, each out of 21) | 21–19 | 17 | 15 | 13 | 11 | 9 | 7 | 6–0 |
| 10% (M, out of 7) | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 2–0 |

Because of the "primacy of Clarity of Purpose" rule, the C and L marks can never be higher than the P mark.

MS2025 Appendix 3 (candidates with a spelling/grammar waiver) gives a worked example that confirms the comparative uses discrete criteria: "The Comparative Study marks, for example, will be applied by awarding marks for PCL: 21 P 19 / 21 C 16 / 21 L 17 = 52" (M is not assessed for these candidates, and the total is then scaled up).

### 30 + 40 split: combined criteria (one mark per part, judged against P/C/L/M)

The parts are **not** split into separate P/C/L/M marks. For each part MS2025 says "Mark ex 30 [or 40] by reference to the criteria for assessment using the breakdown of marks below". It then lists the P, C, L and M descriptors (as above) and gives a single grade line. Appendix 2 lists these grids under **"Combined Criteria"**:

| | H1 | H2 | H3 | H4 | H5 | H6 | H7 | H8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 30 marks, part (a) | 30–27 | 24 | 21 | 18 | 15 | 12 | 9 | 8–0 |
| 40 marks, part (b) | 40–36 | 32 | 28 | 24 | 20 | 16 | 12 | 11–0 |

The same 30 and 40 grids appear in MS2017, MS2019, MS2021, MS2023 and MS2024. Before the 2017 change to H-grades, the schemes used A–E bands. For example, MS2012 has "30 marks A+ 30–26, B 21, C 17, D 12, E- 11–0" and "70 marks A+ 70–60 ... E- 27–0", still split P 21 / C 21 / L 21 / M 7.

### Penalties tied to text choice (MS2025 Appendix 4)

- **No Shakespeare** anywhere on Paper 2 (at HL a Shakespeare play must be either the single text or one of the comparative texts). For a 30/40 question: "disallow the marks awarded for the answer to Question 1.(a) OR disallow 50% of the marks awarded for the answer to Question 1.(b), rounding down". For a 70-mark question: "disallow one third of the mark awarded for this comparative answer, rounded down to the nearest whole number, regardless of whether the candidate has answered using two or three texts in their response." The candidate gets whichever outcome is best for them.
- **Invalid text or incorrect combination of texts** gets the same penalties: lose all of part (a), 50% of part (b), or one third of a 70-mark answer.
- What counts as invalid (MS2020, comparative section): "In answering questions on the Comparative Study, candidates are not permitted to: (i) re-use the Single Text already answered on (ii) use a text not included on the prescribed list of texts for the year in which the examination takes place (iii) use more than one film."
- Every paper from 2016 onward repeats the rule "Candidates may refer to only one film in the course of their answers."

## (c) What examiners reward and what they penalise

### The "GENERAL" note printed before the comparative in every marking scheme, 2012–2026 (wording from MS2025, p. 31; MS2026 repeats it)

> "In all answers to questions in this section, candidates may compare and/or contrast, i.e. address similarities and/or differences in both the content and style of their chosen texts."

> "In shaping their responses to the questions set on the Comparative Study, it is expected that candidates will be involved in some/all of the following kinds of activities:
> - Description/analysis of the text/s in the light of the modes for comparison
> - Making general observations about texts in relation to each other
> - Making connections between similar aspects of texts
> - Recognising differences between texts
> - Showing that similarities/differences need to be qualified
> - Demonstrating awareness of themselves as readers, their reactions/responses/involvement."

> "Expect a wide variety of approaches both in the patterns of discussion and the manner of illustration."

> "In all answers in this section, candidates may refer to one/more key moment/s from the text/s. The purpose of this is to allow the candidates to ground their responses in specific moments without feeling that they must range over the entire text/s. However, do not expect that all the illustrative reference in an answer will come from the key moment/s (unless required). Candidates may offer appropriate illustrative reference from any part of the text/s."

> "Candidates should reference the required combination of texts specified in the questions." (in every scheme from MS2014 onward)

### Instructions repeated question by question

- **Quality of the comparison:** "Pay particular attention to the quality of the comparative writing and the development of the response, including the effective use of apt reference to the texts." (MS2025, every 40- and 70-mark comparative question. A 30-mark part instead says "the quality of the discussion and the development of the response".)
- **Staying in the mode:** P is judged on "evidence of understanding of the mode ..." and "evidence of effective comparison within the mode ..." (MS2025).
- **Comparative language and a sustained comparative focus** are named explicitly under C and L: "sustained comparative focus", "use of comparative language" (MS2025).
- **Covering every element of the question:** schemes spell out the parts that must be answered. For example, "Candidates must deal with more than one interesting lesson" (MS2025, Theme or Issue Q2), and "Allow a broad interpretation of a 'central character' but expect candidates to focus on one character in their discussion" (MS2025, Theme or Issue Q1(a)). Key words in the question are usually given a "broad interpretation", e.g. "Allow for a broad interpretation of 'resilience'" (MS2022), but the candidate still has to deal with them.
- **Part (b) can stand alone:** "Candidates are not required to make comparative links with the text discussed in part (a). However, they may choose to refer in an explicit or implicit way to the points made in their answer to part (a)." (MS2025; the same wording appears in MS2015.)
- **Disagreeing is allowed:** indicative material is "not exhaustive and all appropriate valid answers should be marked according to their merits" (MS2025, p. 2), and every list of points ends "Etc."

### Chief Examiner's Reports (quoted via leavingcertenglish.net, see the note at the top)

2013 report, comparative study. The site says explicitly that this passage "was included in the 2013 report":
> "Many examiners reported genuine engagement with the terms of the questions, combined with a fluid comparative approach. As in previous years, examiners also noted that a significant minority of candidates were hampered by a rigid and formulaic approach."

The site gives the next four quotations, under its "Paper 2 – comparative study" and closing remarks, as coming from "the report". The post is about the 2013 report, but it does not give a year for each of these quotations:
> "it is possible for candidates to challenge, wholly or in part, not only the premise put forward in questions but also the views and opinions they encountered in the course of studying texts"

> "examiners were pleased when they saw candidates trust in their own personal response and demonstrate a willingness to challenge the 'fixed meaning' of texts. The best answers managed to remain grounded, both in the question asked and in the texts"

> "candidates taking examinations in Leaving Certificate English should be prepared to refer to three texts in answer to questions on Paper 2, Section Two, Comparative Studies"

> "All candidates are reminded of the practical imperative of managing their time during the examination carefully and of the need to complete all of the requisite elements of the examination papers"

The teacher's site also says the 2013 report criticised candidates who brought pre-prepared answers they would not adapt to the question. That is the site's paraphrase, not a direct quotation.

2008 report (screenshot of the report on the same page):
> "In the Comparative Section, examiners were impressed by evidence of strong textual knowledge and also the variety of approaches to the analysis of texts. Weaker responses tended to be characterised by an over-rehearsed formulaic approach"

> "Students need to reflect on the particular demands and nuances of questions to achieve a clear focus and structure. They should be encouraged to be flexible in adapting their knowledge to the specific requirements of the question"

> "Students should be aware of the dangers of unfocused narrative particularly in their response to a Single Text question. It is important to move beyond mere description of content"

**Narrative summary:** the official marking schemes do not use the word "summary" in the comparative section. The closest official statements against retelling the plot are the 2008 report's warning about "unfocused narrative" and "mere description of content" quoted above, and GUIDE (p. 76): "Asking students to give summaries of the action in each chapter would not be an appropriate assignment."

## (d) Official guidance on the modes

### The syllabus (SYL §6.4–6.6, pp. 17–18)

> "A comparative study of three or more texts; the emphasis in this study area will be on attitudes, values, structures and styles. At Higher Level the texts can be compared on the basis of the following: a theme or issue; a historical or literary period; a literary genre; the cultural context; the general vision and viewpoint. For each course, three of these comparative modes will be prescribed to give a definite structure and direction to the comparative study. There will be questions on two of the modes in the examination."

> "At Higher Level the study of a film can be included in a comparative study, but three written texts at least must also be studied."
> "At Higher Level the study of a Shakespearean drama is compulsory. This may be chosen either for study as a single text or as an element in the comparative study of a group of texts."

**Exam practice since 2021:** the syllabus says questions are set on two modes, but every main paper from 2021 to 2026 offered **three** mode sections (A, B, C), and so did the deferred papers from 2022 to 2025. In 2025, whose prescribed HL modes were the same three as 2027's, the paper set Theme or Issue, Cultural Context and General Vision and Viewpoint. There is no official statement that this will continue in 2027.

### The 2027 circular (CIRC, Circular 0021/2025, 27 February 2025)

> "Three other texts in a comparative manner, according to the comparative modes prescribed for this course."
> "Any texts from the list of texts prescribed for comparative study, other than the one already chosen for study on its own, may be selected for the comparative study."
> "At Higher Level and at Ordinary Level, a film may be studied as one of the three texts in a comparative study. Only the texts identified on the list as films may be studied as films."
> "The Comparative Modes for Examination in 2027 are: Higher Level (i) Theme or issue (ii) Cultural Context (iii) General Vision and Viewpoint"
> "At Higher Level a play by Shakespeare must be one of the texts chosen. This can be studied on its own or as an element in a comparative study."

### What each mode means (GUIDE, *Approaches to the Comparative Study of Texts*, pp. 71–73)

The PDF is an OCR scan with broken spacing; the spacing has been fixed below and the wording is otherwise unchanged.

- **Theme or issue:** "Comparing texts on a prescribed theme(s). These would have to be themes which were pervasive and central to the texts chosen for study" (examples given: power, love, war/violence).
- **The cultural context:** "Compare texts focusing on social rituals, values, and attitudes. This is not to be seen as a sociological study of the texts. It means taking some perspectives which enable the students to understand the kind of values and structures with which people contend. It amounts to entering into the world of the text and getting some insight and feel for the cultural texture of the world created. This would imply considering such aspects as the rituals of life and the routines of living the structures of society, familial, social, economic, religious and political: the respective roles of men and women in society, the position of children, the role and nature of work, the sources and structures of power and the significance of race and class." (Its list of example groupings includes *Othello*.)
- **General vision and viewpoint:** "Compare texts from the standpoint of the view they offer on life. Is it optimistic or pessimistic? What aspects of life do they concentrate on and why? Evaluate the coherence of the viewpoints as presented in the texts".
- The same section says (p. 73): "The modes of comparison should be seen as specific pathways through texts which will give a clear purpose to each re-reading."

### General Vision and Viewpoint as defined in the marking schemes

> "The term, The General Vision and Viewpoint, may be understood by candidates to mean the broad outlook of the authors of the texts and/or of the texts themselves as interpreted and understood by the reader." (MS2012, p. 25; repeated as "Note 3" in MS2017 and MS2019)

Every mode section on every paper (main and deferred) has exactly two options: one two-part 30 + 40 question and one single 70-mark question. Either can be printed as Q1. In 2012–2014 some asked for "at least two" or "two or more".
